Course Description

Introduction to AI in business. Use of generative AI tools for text, image, and data. Prompt design and interaction strategies. Data reliability, bias, and ethical concerns. Applications in human-AI collaboration and decision-making. Protocol development for responsible and effective AI use. Scenario-based testing and refinement of AI protocols, including agentic GPT design.

Course Objectives

By the end of the semester, students will be able to:

  1. Identify core functions of AI systems and their business applications.
  2. Demonstrate effective use of generative AI tools in diverse business domains.
  3. Apply prompt engineering strategies to improve AI responses.
  4. Analyze how data quality and bias affect AI output.
  5. Explain ethical, legal, and social considerations in business AI use.
  6. Interpret AI outputs in the context of human decision-making.
  7. Construct protocol-based interaction strategies for working with AI.
  8. Design and test agentic GPTs using protocol-driven system instructions.

How This Course Works: Thread-Centered Pedagogy

This course is taught through Thread-Centered Pedagogy. In every session you open a conversation with HokieAI, work through a sequence of structured blocks on the course companion, and export the complete conversation as your submission. The thread captures your conversation as a record of your work. The requirements and evaluation procedures below apply to every thread you submit.

You write your own prompts. The companion provides structure. Write using your own words in every block that asks you to interrogate an idea, respond to an answer, or reflect on the session. The reflection that closes each session records your own thinking, and a reflection drafted by HokieAI will be treated as work that is not your own.

All of your work stays in the thread. Work completed outside the thread cannot be assessed. Do not delete turns, edit messages you have already sent, or reconstruct a conversation after the session has ended. A thread that records where you became stuck and how you corrected course receives full credit for the reasoning it documents.

The thread is transparent and traceable. Each submission records the prompts you wrote, the responses you received, and the actions you took afterward. A complete thread documents the origin of every element of the work. This course encourages good habits for honest AI-assisted work.

How submissions are evaluated. Each block is assessed against the instruction printed above it on the companion page, and every graded element produces a distribution across the class. A thread that was not genuinely created falls outside the range of authentic submissions in those distributions.

If a submission is not genuine. A first instance receives zero points for that submission. Any further instance is reported to the Undergraduate Honor System. A student who disputes a case will be given a comparable but independent assignment to complete under direct supervision. If the quality of that interaction differs substantially from the disputed submission, the case is reported to the Honor System with the supervised result submitted alongside the original.

Lectures and Classwork

All lectures are in person and hands-on unless announced otherwise. Session material appears on the companion at the start of class. You work through the blocks during the meeting, export your thread, and upload it to the Canvas assignment before you leave. Twenty-four graded sessions run from week 2 through week 13 at 1.5 points each.

Homework Assignments

Nine homework assignments are given, one for each of weeks 2 through 10, each worth 3 points. Each homework assignment is released at the end of the day on Thursday and is due at 11:59 pm on the Friday of the following week. Homework follows the same structure as the classwork and is usually more substantial, and some assignments require more than one thread. Homework is individual work. You are encouraged to discuss homework with your classmates. A submission that reproduces another student's thread in whole or in part will not receive credit. A submission up to 24 hours late is subject to a 10 percent penalty and receives no credit after that. Once a homework grade is released, you have one week to raise a question about it.

Project

Project work begins with Phase III. Teams form informally in week 10 and are finalized in week 11. Each team designs and builds an agentic system for an authentic use case. Three deliverables are due during the semester at 10 points each, and each team presents its system during week 14 for 10 points. Presentations are evaluated by the students' peers. Full project instructions are released in week 11.

Email

Put BUS 1204 and the purpose of your message in the subject line so that it is filtered correctly. Open with "Hi Dr. Seref" or "Dear Dr. Seref", keep the body brief, and sign your name at the end. Messages that do not follow this format will not receive a response.

Absences from Class

A student who has to miss a lecture for a documentable reason can request an extension on that session's classwork until 11:59 pm the same day. Request it by email to seref@vt.edu at least 24 hours before the lecture begins. Requests after that deadline will not be considered. Submit your supporting document to the Excuse Document Submission assignment on Canvas as a PDF. Official activities such as school-related travel and professional activities such as job interviews are valid reasons, recognized on submission of a letter or email from the relevant authority or organization. For a health problem, notify the instructor by email before the lecture where possible and no later than three days after it, with documentation submitted within the same three days. Other emergencies are handled case by case and should be raised as soon as possible. Personal preferences, including family travel and leaving early for or returning late from a break, are not valid reasons. You receive an extension with an accepted excuse, and you still need to complete the work by the extended deadline to earn credit.
