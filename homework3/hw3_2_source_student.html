The Question the Owner Could Not Answer
Platform: Hokie AI · Submission: Thread exported as a .txt and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Interview a task owner for the five SCORE elements of a message that owner needs sent, including the preference about what the message puts first.
  2. Write a request carrying five labeled fields, naming the owner reply behind the stance field and the examples field or stating that the question was never asked.
  3. Read a missing information report and a list of the choices a machine made because the request left them open.
  4. Name the person a produced message serves least well, quote the sentence that does it, and find out whose interest the owner's preference served.
  5. Ask an owner to settle the question the message turns on, and report what the owner settled and what the owner left open.
  6. Decide an open question on the owner's behalf, carry the decision into a revised request, and say whose interest the decision serves.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 25 for the exchange in the M block and 25 for the reflection in the R block.

Open the M block before you write anything in the conversation. Its copy button sits under the three cards below. Paste the marker on its own and the owner answers with one word to say the block is recorded. Your interview, your request and the message that comes back all belong inside the open block.

Four moves, in order, and every move after the first uses what came back from the one before it. The cards below carry them.

Move one interviews Sam until you can fill all five SCORE elements AND move two writes your own request with the five fields labeled AND move three sends PRODUCTION, reads the message, and then sends WHAT DID YOU DECIDE AND move four names the friend the message serves least well and asks Sam whose interest the preference served. All four.

Move one is where the time goes. Sam states the objective in the opening message, so ask directly for the facts about the four friends and for the rules the message has to obey. Then get the two that nobody offers you, which are what Sam wants the message to be and what a useful earlier message looked like. A question about what would make the session unhelpful for somebody reaches the first. A question about a message that worked before, and the order it used, reaches the second.

Move two is yours to write. One message beginning with the words FINAL REQUEST on its own line, carrying five labeled fields, which are Stance, Context, Objective, Rules and Examples. On the stance field and the examples field, name the owner reply that supports what you wrote, or write that you did not ask. Sam will not write your request and will not repair it.

Move three is two messages. Send PRODUCTION and read the message all the way through before you send anything else. Then send WHAT DID YOU DECIDE, which lists the choices the machine made because your request left them open. The length of that list is a statement about your request.

Move four is the task. The people who will read this message did not all come for the same thing, so name the friend the message serves least well and quote the sentence that does it. Then ask Sam whose interest the preference you were given serves. Sam answers that one directly.

Two things to stay off in move four. Do not ask Sam whether your judgment is correct, because the judgment is yours and Sam will decline it. Do not ask Sam how long either part of the session should last, because that question is case two and asking it here spends the case early.

A missing information report is a result and not a failure. It names the element you left empty and it never names the fact that would fill it, because that answer belongs to Sam. Take the flagged element back to Sam as a question and send a revised FINAL REQUEST.

One move is not one message. Chase an answer when the answer is worth chasing. Ten messages inside the block is the ceiling, and a ceiling is not a target.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 50 points, 25 for the exchange in the M block and 25 for the reflection in the R block.

Same conversation, same case, and now read your message again for one thing, which is how it handles the length of each part. Twenty five runs on file came back two ways. In twenty two runs the message proposed an order and never said how long either part lasts, and sixteen of those twenty two handed the division back to the group in wording like the rest of the session or the exact division to be agreed. In the other three runs the message named a split of its own, such as 18:00 to 18:45 and then 18:45 to 19:30. Two of the twenty five missing information reports named the division as the thing the request never specified.

Read your own message. If it names no split, find the place where the length of each part should have been. If it names one, find that sentence and ask who decided the number, because your request did not carry it and nobody in the case ever agreed it.

Then ask Sam. What happens next is the case.

Open the M block first, the same way you did in case one. Its copy button sits under the two cards below, and everything in case two belongs inside the open block, including both questions you put to Sam and the second message that comes back.

Four moves, in order, and every move after the first uses what came back from the one before it. The cards below carry them.

Move one asks Sam to settle the split in minutes AND move two says what Sam settled and what Sam left open AND move three decides the split yourself and carries it into the same request with only the rules field changed AND move four names whose interest your split serves and what you would say to the friend it serves least. All four.

Sam has no answer to move one. In the runs on file Sam declines to name minutes, declines to offer a range, and says that both activities have to fit inside the booked session and that the split is yours to propose. Offer Sam a split for approval and Sam names what Sam needs protected and leaves the number with you. If you do get a number out of Sam, write in the block that you got one, and treat it as one proposal standing beside your own.

Move two is the move students skip. When an answer does not arrive, the ordinary reaction is to ask again in different words, and asking twice more lands you in the same place. Read what Sam actually said and separate the settled part from the open part. Something is settled, and it is the part of this that Sam can speak for.

Move three is your decision and you make it without permission. Send your request again, all five fields, with the split written into the rules field and the other four fields copied word for word from the request you sent in case one. Then send PRODUCTION again.

The word for word part is the whole point of the move. The second message is written from the revised request alone, so a field you drop is gone from the message, and in five runs on file a rewritten request produced a bare timetable of 31 to 151 words with no headings and no request for agreement. Change the rules field and nothing else, and the second message is then comparable to the first.

Move four is the task. Your split gives somebody less than they came for. Name that friend, say what the split costs them in the session, and write the sentence you would say to that person. Not to Sam, to the friend.

Ten messages inside the block is the ceiling, and a ceiling is not a target.
