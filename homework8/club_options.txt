Larkspur Investment Club, savings options for first-year students

The Larkspur Investment Club is a student club made up for a class exercise. The club helps first-year students think about saving and investing. It offers education, not advice, and it does not recommend specific investments.

Option 1. Savings at the Treasury Bills rate. A high-yield savings account pays a rate that moves with short-term interest rates. The club uses the latest average interest rate on Treasury Bills, published by the U.S. Treasury, as its guide to what safe savings can earn today. The rate changes every month.

Option 2. A certificate. A certificate pays a fixed rate for a set time. The club's rate sheet, dated 2026-10-01, uses 3.75 percent a year.

Option 3. A stock index fund. A stock index fund owns many companies' shares. Its value goes up and down, and in some years it falls. The club board's assumption for projections is an average of 7.0 percent a year. This is an assumption, not a promise, and only the club board may change it.

How the club projects savings. Every projection starts with the amount a student puts in now, adds the same deposit every month for a number of years, and compares the three options with the club's projection script. The script, its assumptions and any change to them are approved by the club board.
