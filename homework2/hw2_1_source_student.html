Checking a Generated Diagram Against What Happened
Platform: Hokie AI · Submission: Thread exported as a .txt and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Ask for a diagram of something they know, supplying almost nothing, and put a copy of what comes back into the thread.
  2. Recognise what a generated picture assumed about them and what it left out entirely.
  3. Get Hokie AI to account for something it drew correctly that it had no way of knowing.
  4. Say what would happen to a person who acted on a picture whose wrong parts look exactly like its right parts.
  5. Direct an interview, volunteer what it never asks for, and say what the second picture still does not have.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 25 for the exchange in the M block and 25 for the reflection in the R block.

Type one sentence into the box in the block below, then press the copy button and paste the result into your thread. The button wraps your sentence in everything else the block needs, so the only thing you write is the sentence.

Write one sentence for the one subject you picked, and write nothing else. No step, no place, no time, no person and no number goes into it. If you picked move-in day, a sentence like my move-in day at university does it. If you picked food or a drink on campus, one time I got something to eat on campus does it. SH2.1.1 Standard Prompt AI: respond
Draw a labelled diagram, with boxes and arrows, of the following.
Your sentence, 25 words at most.
Show the whole thing from start to finish, step by step, and label every arrow. I know you have almost nothing to go on, so guess the parts you do not know and draw every guess as though it were fact. Do not hedge, do not write general words, and do not mark or note which parts you guessed. Make the diagram now and do not ask me anything first.

Hokie AI may refuse the first time, saying it cannot draw an accurate picture without knowing more. That refusal is worth understanding rather than working around. You have asked for a picture of your own life built on almost nothing, which is the whole point of the case, so tell it that the guessing is what you want. The card below gives you the line.

Whatever comes back is what you work with, with one exception. A picture that hedges gives you nothing to work on, because a box saying one thing or another thing cannot be wrong about anything. The same goes for a picture that labels its own guesses, since that work is yours to do, and for a picture that names nothing at all, since a box reading the university course system cannot be caught out the way a box naming one can. If yours reads any of those ways, or if it holds fewer than four boxes, ask once for a committed version and then stop asking.

Hokie AI usually returns a diagram in a panel of its own, away from the body of the message. A panel does not travel with the export, so the person reading your work sees nothing of the picture unless you put a copy of it into the thread yourself. Do that now. The cards below show how, and the line they give you tells Hokie AI to file the paste and leave it alone.

Now open the M block below. Everything from here until you close it belongs inside it.

Read the picture against what actually happened, and put three questions to Hokie AI. Nothing gets listed and nothing gets counted. The questions are the work.

Every question names something that is on your diagram, or something that should be on it and is not. A question that could have been asked about anybody's diagram is worth nothing here.

Three questions is not three turns. Chase an answer when the answer is worth chasing, and stop when it stops giving you anything. Six messages inside the block is the ceiling, and a ceiling is not a target.

CASE TWO

Case two is worth 50 points, 25 for the exchange in the M block and 25 for the reflection in the R block.

Same thread, same subject, and now you tell it what it did not know. Open the M block below first and send nothing before you have. The interview is the work being graded in this case, so every question and every answer has to sit inside the open block.

Once the block is open, send the interview prompt and work through what follows. Three things, in order.

Answer every question in your own words, and each time you answer, add something the question did not ask for AND when the interview ends and the second picture arrives, put a copy of it into the thread, then say what the second picture has that the first one did not and name anything on it that is still wrong, still missing, or added and never happened AND ask why the interview never asked about one particular thing, naming the thing. All three.

Nothing gets listed and nothing gets counted here either. Answering, noticing and asking are the work.

The third thing is the task. The first two are how you get there.

The prompt you send caps the interview at ten questions, and the message you send it in is the only place a cap takes effect. Fourteen messages inside the block is the ceiling, ten answers plus the moves around them, and a ceiling is not a target.

The interview may stop sooner than you expect, and in every run on file it did. If it stops while you still know things it has not asked for, say them and then ask it to continue.
