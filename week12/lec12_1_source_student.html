Week 12 · Lecture 1
The Team Website and the Embedded Agent
Duration: 75 minutes · Platform: Hokie AI and Google Sites · Submission: Your reflection thread, exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas before leaving class · Lecture Mode: consolidation
Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students will be able to:

  1. Build and publish a Google Site that shows a business's facts and holds its customer agent.
  2. Check that every fact on a business's website matches the fact sheet the business's agents use.
  3. Use Hokie AI to turn a personal taste into a website's colors, fonts, logo and banner image.
  4. Start the team's own website for progress report 1.
Section 1 of 4
INTRODUCTION
📋 Note for Students

Today you build a small website for Kestrel Bend Outfitters with your instructor, step by step. Then your team designs and starts the website for its own business, which your team needs for progress report 1.

A customer of Kestrel Bend Outfitters finds the business on its website. The customer reads the trips, the prices and the rules there, and asks the trip desk a question in a box on the same page. You start with a practice version of that website.

A business's website has four parts. The facts are the business's trips or products, its prices, its hours and its rules, all copied from the fact sheet AND the agent is the customer agent in a box on the page, where customers ask their questions AND the look is the colors, fonts, logo and banner image that fit the business's customers AND the address is the published web address, which anyone can open. Four parts.

The customer agent knows only the fact sheet. If the website shows a price that is missing from the fact sheet, a customer gets one answer from the page and another from the agent. So every fact on the website comes from the fact sheet.

Your practice site takes six steps, in this order.

First, open sites.google.com/new. Google Sites shows a row of templates. Click Blank site, the large plus sign at the start of the row. A new site opens in the editor, with a panel of tabs on the right.

THEN name your site. Click Untitled site at the top left and type Kestrel Bend Practice. Then click the large page title in the middle and type the same name. Everyone in the room can use this name, because each site belongs to its own account.

THEN add the facts. Download the Kestrel Bend fact sheet from the box above, open it, and copy all of it. In the panel on the right, open the Insert tab and click Text box. A box appears on the page. Paste the fact sheet into it. You can make the section titles bold with the text tools above the box.

THEN embed the trip desk. Copy the trip desk link from the box above. In the Insert tab, click Embed, paste the link into the By URL box, and click Insert. A box appears with the Hokie AI sign in page in it. Drag the corners of the box until it is about as wide as the page and tall enough for a conversation.

THEN publish. Click the blue Publish button at the top right. Type a web address that ends with your VT username, such as kestrel-bend-practice-jsmith. Every web address under vt.edu belongs to one site only, and your username keeps yours different from your classmates'. If Google Sites says the address is not available, change it and try again. Choose the public setting, so that anyone can open the site without a VT sign in, and click Publish. A change you make later appears on the published site only after you click Publish again and reload the published page with Ctrl+F5, or Command+Shift+R on a Mac.

THEN ask. Open your published site in a new tab. The box shows the Hokie AI sign in page, so sign in there with your VT account. Then send the question in the box below.

Section 3 of 4
PROJECT WORK

Now your team designs the website for its own business. Your team's look takes three steps.

First, each of you proposes a look of your own, so your team has several to choose from. Open a new regular Hokie AI thread. You do not submit this thread. Attach your team's fact sheet, which is in the box of fact sheet buttons on the progress report 1 page, and send the look prompt below. Write two or three words about the feel you like in place of the words in brackets. Hokie AI answers with three colors as hex codes, two fonts, a tagline and a reason. Then send the logo prompt in the same thread, and check that the business name in the logo is spelled exactly as on the fact sheet.

THEN your team chooses. Show each other your looks and logos on your laptops, and pick one as a team, or mix two, such as one member's colors with another member's logo.

THEN your team's builder builds the look into the team's website, with the steps further down this page. The member whose look the team picked sends the banner prompt for a wide image without text.

Class material
The look prompt
I attached the fact sheet of my team's made up business. My team is building the business's website in Google Sites. Suggest one look for the site that fits the business's customers and my own taste, which is: [write two or three words about the feel you like, such as calm and earthy, or bold and playful]. Give three colors as hex codes for a Google Sites custom theme (a background, a main color and an accent), one font for headings and one for normal text from Google Fonts, a tagline of no more than eight words, and one sentence on why the look fits the customers. Do not add any fact that is not on the fact sheet.
Attach your team's fact sheet in the same message.
Class material
The logo prompt
Make a simple square logo for this look: the business name spelled exactly as on the fact sheet, the main color and the accent color, and one small picture that fits the business. Plain background, no other text.
Press the download button on the image in Hokie AI's reply to save it to your laptop.
Class material
The banner prompt
Make a wide banner image for the top of the website in this look, about three times wider than tall, with no text in it.
Only the member whose look the team picks sends this prompt.

Your team's website takes five steps. The builder works on one laptop, and the rest of the team sits beside the builder and helps.

First, the builder opens sites.google.com/new, clicks Blank site, and names the site with your business's name, at the top left and as the page title.

THEN the builder shares the site. Click Share with others, the person icon with a plus sign next to Publish, and add each member's VT email, so every member can edit the site later.

THEN the builder sets the look. Open the Themes tab on the right and find the Custom section. Click the plus sign to create a theme. The window asks for three colors, a background, a main color and an accent, so paste those three hex codes from the look your team picked. For the fonts, open the font list and click More fonts to find the two fonts by name. The same window takes the logo and the banner image, the wide image at the top of the page. Save the theme.

THEN the team writes the facts page. Copy your business's offers, prices, hours and rules from your fact sheet into text boxes on the page. Hokie AI may rewrite them as friendlier web text, but check every price, hour and rule against the fact sheet, and delete any fact it adds.

THEN the builder publishes. Click Publish, type a web address with your business's name and your team number, such as copper-ladle-team03, choose the public setting, and click Publish. Copy the address and send it to the team.

Part B of progress report 1 asks for your team's website address on the third line. Your team's site shows the facts from your fact sheet and holds your customer agent in a box, the way your practice site holds the trip desk. Your team does not need to finish today. Before you leave, your team has the site, its look, the start of its facts page and the address, and knows what is left.

BUS 1204 AI Literacy for Business, Week 12 Lecture 1. You are an AI assistant for this session. The student built a website in Google Sites for a made up business and is about to paste a reflection on it for the record. Acknowledge the reflection in one sentence, and do not analyze or comment on its content unless the student asks a question. --- **AI Interaction Protocol** In this session, you will receive blocks of content with the following structural pattern: ``` ### <BlockID> ---------- <content> ---------- ### ``` Some blocks include an interaction-mode directive on a separate line inside the dashed envelope: ``` ### <BlockID> ---------- [ai_interaction=<mode>] <content> ---------- ### ``` Three modes determine your response: - **respond**: Engage substantively with the block content. Treat it as a prompt, question, or task that wants your analytical response. Default for S blocks (prompts) and X blocks (free exploration). - **acknowledge**: Confirm receipt with one brief sentence naming the block ID. Do not analyze, comment on, or expand on the content. The student is filing the block for the record, not asking for engagement. Default for T blocks (this opener), R blocks (reflections), M CLOSE blocks (closing markers), Q blocks (structured selections), and O blocks (ordering selections). - **archive**: Respond with only the block ID and nothing else. Used for batched archival pastes where even brief acknowledgment would clutter the thread. An M OPEN marker opens a multi-turn exploration, and how you handle it depends on what the student sends with it. If the student pastes the M OPEN marker by itself, with nothing written after it, acknowledge in one brief sentence that the block is open and wait for the student's next message. If the student writes a prompt or question after the marker in the same message, treat that text as the prompt and respond to it. Always acknowledge an M CLOSE marker. If a block has no explicit `[ai_interaction=...]` directive, apply the block-type default per the list above. This protocol applies for the remainder of this session. ---
