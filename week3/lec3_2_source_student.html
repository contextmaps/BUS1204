Week 3 · Lecture 2
Building a Business Visual Over Several Turns
Duration: 75 minutes · Platform: Hokie AI · Submission: Thread exported as a .txt and uploaded to Canvas before leaving class · Lecture Mode: consolidating
Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students will be able to:

  1. Name what a request leaves for an AI to decide, and say which of those decisions only they can make.
  2. Ask an AI what it needs to know before it produces anything, push it for more than its first question, and answer in their own words.
  3. Supply real figures to a request that did not ask for them, and say what the picture gained.
  4. Run the read-back check on a generated chart and report what it found, whichever way it lands.
  5. Ask what further information would improve a picture, supply it, and say what changed.
Section 1 of 5
INTRODUCTION
📋 Note for Students

On Tuesday you counted the things a picture claimed that nobody had supplied. The count was never zero, and the reason was always the same. You had not told it, so it decided.

Today you tell it. You will build one picture three times in one conversation, and each time you will have given it something it did not have before. The last version will be about your decision, for your audience, using figures you provided. Nothing about the tool changes across those three versions. What changes is what it knows, and you are the only source of that.

One conversation today, not three. You will stop three times inside it, and at each stop you will have a picture. What separates the three pictures is what you told it between them.

S3.2.3 Standard Prompt AI: respond
Our university career centre runs four services, resume reviews, mock interviews, drop-in advising and employer information sessions, and we track appointments by month from September to February. Some services grew over that period and some declined. Create a line chart for the centre's annual review that shows how the four services moved across the six months. Add a clear takeaway headline.

Now open the M block and stay in the same thread for the rest of the hour. Three stops, and you have already reached the first one.

Six months of appointments for the four services. These are not the numbers your instructor used, and this is not the company from the demonstration. Do not paste them into your first message. Hold them until Hokie AI asks, because what it asks for is part of what you are here to see.

Stay in one thread and reach all three stops. The three cards above carry the moves and not the words, because a sentence you copied is not a decision you made.

On the way to Stop 1 you will ask it to interview you to improve the chart. Point the interview at something, because an interview with no destination gets you polling questions. Say one question at a time in the same message, or you will get a block of seven at once and answer none of them properly. Then answer what it asks in your own words, and hand over your figures at the moment it asks for them.

The fourth box is the large card, and it is part of the same leg. Ask for a version aimed at the leads for resume reviews and employer information sessions, which are the two services that fall across the six months. Run the read-back check on that version before you accept it. Resume reviews and mock interviews cross over between January and February, so there is somewhere real for a drawing fault to hide. Say what your check found either way, because a clean check is an answer and roughly half of you will get one.

Your checked picture is Stop 1.

The leg to Stop 2 is yours and the page stops helping. Ask what one more piece of information from you would make the chart more useful, because a question it can answer with a yes or a no gets a no. Then answer what it names, and expect more than one. On a sampled run it asked four things in a row, a benchmark, an audience, an action and a contact line, and two of those were things nobody had thought to supply. Keep answering until it makes the picture again, and that picture is Stop 2. If it names nothing at all, write that down and choose something yourself.

Then put the new picture beside Stop 1 and go looking. On a sampled run the only thing asked for was brand colours, and the chart came back with a truncated axis, a headline demoted to a subtitle and two percentage callouts gone. None of that was requested and none of it was announced. Naming what moved that you never asked for is the last thing this hour asks of you. All three pictures stay in this one thread, because your export is what gets read later.

Section 5 of 5
REFLECTION

Now write your reflection, in your own words and without help from the AI.

Your reflection needs all four of these. Name one thing you supplied that Hokie AI could not have known, and say what the picture did with it AND say what your read-back check found, including a clear statement if it found nothing wrong AND say what you chose to add at Stop 2 and why you chose that one out of everything you could have asked for AND say which of your three pictures you would put your name on and send to somebody who cannot see the figures behind it. Respond to all four.

The fourth is the one that separates a good reflection from an adequate one. Saying that the last picture is the best one is not enough. Say what has to be true about a picture before you would send it, and say whether your last one is actually there yet.

Write 80 to 160 words.
