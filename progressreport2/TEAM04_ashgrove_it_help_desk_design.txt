ASHGROVE ACCOUNTING GROUP IT HELP DESK, SYSTEM DESIGN FOR PROGRESS REPORT 2
TEAM04. Ashgrove Accounting Group is made up, and so are the employees, systems and names below. This design builds on your fact sheet from progress report 1 and on the Kestrel Bend system from lec13_1. Every box of text marked COPY is pasted exactly as it is.


1. YOUR BUSINESS IN SHORT

Ashgrove Accounting Group is a company of forty employees in Christiansburg with its own IT help desk. Employees report problems with a computer, an account or a printer, and ask for software or equipment. Your customer agent takes two kinds of request: a ticket for a problem, and a purchase request for software or equipment. Your staff agent works with Priya Natarajan, the IT manager, who decides every request. Coverage is thin in holiday weeks, so your staff agent checks the public holiday calendar, and the IT manager uses it to plan who is on call.

The system has five parts:
Services tab: the company's systems, whether each one is working, and who is on call. Both agents read it.
Requests tab: one row per request, with its status and the IT manager's note. Only the staff agent reads it.
Customer agent: reads the Services tab and adds a request to the Requests tab. It holds no office key.
Staff agent: lists the requests, checks the Services tab and the holiday calendar, and records the IT manager's decision after the IT manager confirms.
Priya Natarajan: decides every request and updates the Services tab by hand.

7. THE IT MANAGER'S DECISIONS

Priya Natarajan decides every request. Before each decision, the staff agent checks what the fact sheet and the Services tab say, and the IT manager decides.

A ticket. The staff agent checks the priority against the fact sheet (urgent when a department cannot work, a virus or phishing is suspected, or a client deadline is today; high when one employee cannot work; normal otherwise), whether the employee tried the fix the fact sheet lists, and whether the Services tab already shows the problem. The IT manager assigns the ticket to a technician, named in the note, or declines it, for example when the fix on the fact sheet solves the problem. Moving a ticket ahead of its priority level is an exception, and the IT manager decides it with a note.
After an assigned ticket about a new problem with a service: the IT manager changes that service's status, note and updated time on the Services tab, so the customer agent can tell the next employee about it.

A purchase. The staff agent checks whether the software is on the approved list, and whether equipment costs more than 300 dollars, which needs the department head as well. The IT manager approves or declines. Software outside the approved list or a personal device for work is an exception, and the IT manager decides it with a note.

A holiday week. When the IT manager asks about holidays, the staff agent calls get_holidays and reports the holidays of the coming months. The IT manager plans who is on call in those weeks and updates the on call row, SV-06, on the Services tab by hand, with the person's name in status.

9. WHAT YOUR INSTRUCTIONS NEED

Your agents now write to and read from the sheet, so your team updates the SCORE instructions it wrote for progress report 1. Write them yourselves, on the pattern of the Kestrel Bend instructions in lec13_1. The add_request call takes the place of the HANDOFF line in the customer agent, and decide_request takes the place of the RECORD line in the staff agent.

The customer agent's instructions need to say:
Call read_services before answering any question about a problem or a system, answer only from the rows it receives, and give the employee any note for that service.
Suggest the fix from the fact sheet first. For a ticket, collect the employee's name, department, the service, the priority and the problem. For a purchase, collect the name, department, the item and its cost if known. Repeat the details back.
When the employee says yes, call add_request in the same reply, once, give the request_id and say that the IT staff confirm every request. Never say that a ticket is assigned or a purchase approved.
The agent never asks for a password. The agent cannot see other requests, and says it can help only with the employee's own new request.

The staff agent's instructions need to say:
Call list_requests when the IT manager asks about requests, with status pending for what is waiting, and report each request with its request_id and details.
Call read_services when the IT manager asks about a service, and get_holidays for holiday weeks.
Record a decision only when the IT manager names a request_id and says assign, approve or decline. Repeat it, ask the IT manager to confirm, then call decide_request once.
After an assigned ticket about a new problem, remind the IT manager to update the Services tab.
