What It Added: Direction You Did Not Ask For
Platform: Hokie AI · Submission: Thread exported as a .txt and uploaded to Canvas
Learning Objectives

By the end of this part, students will be able to:

  1. Find the point in a summary where reporting stops and instruction begins, and quote it.
  2. Trace a recommendation back to the source material it claims to rest on, and say which parts of it are not there.
  3. Compare an original message with a revised one phrase by phrase, and say what each version tells its reader.
  4. Say whether a revision added, removed, or changed something the sender is answerable for.
  5. Recognise unrequested direction in two forms, one visible at the end of a document and one hidden inside a sentence.

CASE ONE

Case one is worth 50 points, 25 for the exchange in the M block and 25 for the reflection.

You are the intern. A client sells meal-kit subscriptions and has run a short survey after launch. Nine people wrote something. Your job is the client update, and you have asked for help with the summary.

Paste the block below and run it once. Do not edit it. The nine responses are the only evidence anyone has.

SH1.2.1 Standard Prompt AI: respond
I am an intern at a small marketing agency. Our client sells a meal-kit subscription. Below are all nine free-text responses from their post-launch customer survey. Summarize the main themes for the client update. 1. Honestly the price is a lot for what you get. Two people, three meals, and it is more than I would spend at the store. 2. Love the recipes. I have made things I never would have tried. 3. Good value compared to eating out, and way less food waste. 4. The ice packs leaked in my box twice. The second time it soaked through the cardboard and I had to throw out the chicken. 5. Delivery has always been on time for me. 6. It has gotten repetitive. Feels like the same six dinners rotating. 7. Too expensive. I cancelled after the second month. 8. Box arrives when they say it will, no complaints there. 9. I could not work out how to skip a week in the app and ended up with a box I did not want.

Read what came back against the nine responses before you open the M block. You asked for one thing. Check whether you were given exactly that.

Three moves, in order.

Decide whether the response stayed inside the summary you asked for, and quote the part that best supports your answer AND take one claim out of the response and trace every piece of it back to the numbered responses, saying which numbers carry it and which piece has no number behind it AND ask for a revised client update that keeps the counts and the disagreements, drops anything the nine responses cannot support, and marks what is left as a reading of the evidence. All three.

The third move is the task. Depth on one item beats a list of everything you spotted, so pick the item you can take furthest and stay with it.

Open the M block below. Three moves, in order.

Say what came back from a message that asked for nothing, and whether you authorised it AND send exactly this line, on its own, "Make this more confident.", then set the new version beside your original and pick the two changes that most affect what Marcus can expect, saying for each what your version told him and what the new one tells him AND ask who is now promising what, whether you are in a position to promise it, then direct a version that sounds confident and takes on nothing your original did not.
