BUS 1204 AI Literacy for Business
Week 10 · Lecture 1
Skills, Instructions That Load Themselves
Duration: 75 minutes · Platform: Hokie AI · Submission: The regular Hokie AI thread, holding the student's skill test log and the reflection, exported as a .zip and uploaded to Canvas before leaving class · Lecture Mode: exploratory
Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students will be able to:

  1. Say what a skill is in Hokie AI and how it differs from a prompt, an agent's instructions and a knowledge file.
  2. Write a skill's description and its body, and say what each one controls.
  3. Tell from a reply whether a skill loaded, by the pill box under it and by Execution Details.
  4. Test a skill on requests that should load it and requests that should not, and change the description from what the tests show.
  5. Judge what a skill made for a whole company should be allowed to change in an employee's replies.
Section 1 of 5
INTRODUCTION
📋 Note for Students

So far you have given Hokie AI instructions three ways. A prompt applies once, because you typed it. An agent's instructions apply inside that agent, because you opened it. A knowledge file is something an agent can look up. Today adds a fourth. A skill is a set of instructions stored in your Hokie AI account, and Hokie AI reads its description to decide, request by request, whether to load it.

You leave today with a skill of your own, for something you keep asking an AI to do your way.

A skill has four fields. The name is what you see in your list. The description says what the skill is for and when it applies, and Hokie AI reads it to decide whether to load the skill. Always On keeps the skill available in every conversation. The body holds the instructions themselves, written in Markdown.

Your instructor keeps a skill called Explain it my way. Its description and its body are below. The body asks for a label at the top of the reply, so you can tell from the reply alone whether the skill shaped it.

Class material

Use when I ask you to explain or teach me a concept from a business course, such as accounting, marketing, finance, economics, or management. Not for writing, editing, or summarizing tasks.

# Explain it my way

When I ask you to explain a concept from one of my business courses:

1. Start with one everyday example I would recognize as a first-year college student.
2. Then give the definition in one or two plain sentences.
3. Then ask me one short question that checks whether I understood, and stop. Do not answer it.

Class material
Customer email house style, the description
Use when the user asks you to draft, write, or reply to an email to a customer or client of a business. Not for emails to friends, professors, or coworkers, and not for text messages or chat replies.
The specific description, the one the skill starts with.
Class material
Customer email house style, the body
# Customer email house style

When you draft or reply to an email to a customer or client of a business:

- Address the customer by name if the request gives one, otherwise open with "Hello,".
- The first sentence answers the customer's question or states the outcome.
- Keep the body to 120 words or fewer.
- Use no exclamation marks.
- Close with "Best regards," and a line for the sender's name.
- After the signature, add this line exactly: [house style v1]
The marker line is the last rule.

Section 4 of 5
DISCUSSION

A skill shapes every reply it loads into, and the reply reads as Hokie AI's own work. The only signs are the pill box and the get_skill_content line in Execution Details, and the exported chat keeps neither. Always On keeps a skill available, and Hokie AI still reads the description, request by request, to decide whether to load it.

💬 Discussion

Take these into the reflection:

  • Which words in a description let a skill into a request it was not meant for?
  • A reader of your email sees the email and never the pill box. Is that a problem when the skill is yours? When your employer wrote it?
  • What should happen when a skill's rule and your own request disagree?

Turn to the person beside you. Read your description to your partner AND have your partner say one request that should load it AND one that should not. Then switch.

Four minutes, both directions.

📋 Note for Students

POTENTIAL USE CASES. Anywhere a person or a business repeats the same instructions to an AI, a skill can hold them once, and the description says where they apply.

A student's skill that turns lecture notes into flash cards in the same format every time.
A student organization's skill that writes event announcements in the club's voice, with the date, place and sign up link always in the same order.
A small business's skill for customer emails, with the refund policy wording the owner approved.
At an internship, a team's skill that writes meeting summaries with decisions, owners and dates, so every summary reads the same.
