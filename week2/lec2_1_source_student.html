AI as Collaborator, Not Decision-Maker: Roles, Strengths, and Limits
Duration: 75 minutes · Platform: Hokie AI · Submission: Thread exported as a .txt and uploaded to Canvas before leaving class · Lecture Mode: exploratory
Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students will be able to:

  1. Distinguish between AI as a collaborator and AI as a decision-maker.
  2. Identify business tasks where AI adds genuine value versus tasks where it should be constrained.
  3. Explain why trust in AI must be calibrated to task type and context, not assumed from output quality.
  4. Construct a prompt that explicitly frames AI as a collaborator and observe how framing changes behavior.
  5. Reflect on a specific moment in their own interaction where the boundary between collaboration and decision-making became visible.
  6. Compare how wording shifted control across two students' interactions with AI.

REFLECTION

Now write your reflection, in your own words and without help from the AI. Choose one specific moment from your exploration where you could see the line between the AI helping you think and the AI making the call.

Your reflection needs all four of these. Say what you asked AND what Hokie AI did AND what that moment showed about who was really in control of the thinking AND why you think your wording produced that difference. Respond to all four in your reflection.

The fourth is the one that separates a good reflection from an adequate one, and it is the one most people skip. Saying that the wording mattered is not enough. Say what about your wording did it.

Write 80 to 160 words about that one moment, not a summary of the whole conversation.
